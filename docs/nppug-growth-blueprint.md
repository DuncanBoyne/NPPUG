# NPPUG Growth Blueprint

**An evidence-backed positioning, website, and community-growth strategy for Norfolk Power Platform User Group**

- Prepared: 8 July 2026
- Method: Tavily web search + full-page extraction across 20 strong and weak Microsoft/tech communities (UK + global), plus first-party operator evidence from Microsoft MVP community-building guides, cross-checked against NPPUG's current site, Meetup landscape, and Microsoft Community presence.
- Audience: this document is written to be handed to another AI model, a website builder, or a community organiser. Every recommendation cites the community it was observed in.

---

## 1. Executive summary

The strongest Microsoft-ecosystem communities do not win because they describe themselves better. They win because they **show proof instead of describing intent**: a named next event above the fold, real photos, real numbers, named sponsors with named benefits, a "what to expect at your first meetup" promise, and a speaker/sponsor pipeline with a self-serve form. The weak communities all share the same failure: **generic aspirational copy ("connect, learn, share, grow") with no proof, no next event, and no capture mechanism.**

The single most important finding for NPPUG: the dormant "Norfolk Microsoft Fabric - Power BI User Group" on Meetup (24 members, zero events ever held) uses nearly the same vocabulary as NPPUG's current homepage hero — "learn, share, and grow together… a friendly, supportive environment." Words alone do not distinguish a living community from a dead one. **Proof does.** NPPUG has real proof (monthly meetups, 10 speakers, a co-founded regional summit, 385+ LinkedIn followers) but its website currently leads with the generic words and buries the proof.

## 3. The patterns: what strong communities do that weak ones don't

### Pattern 1 — Lead with the next event, not the mission
Every living group's page is dominated by a *named, dated, located* next event with named speakers and full abstracts (PBIMCR, London FabUG, D365PPUG, LBAG). Every dead or dying group leads with mission language and shows "No upcoming events" (Norfolk Fabric ghost group, SPPUG) or "Date TBC" (North East Power BI). **The next event is the product; the mission is the packaging.**

### Pattern 2 — Proof beats promises, in a specific order
The strong groups' proof stack, in the order it appears on their pages:
1. **Numbers**: DUG "50,000 members" H1; DataGrillen stats banner (200/52/4/2); D365PPUG counter section (groups / meetings per year / YouTube subscribers / social followers); LBAG "4.7★ · 2,794 ratings · 201 past events".
2. **Photos of real humans at real events**: nor(DEV) gallery + "Checkout 2025's Photo Reel"; D365PPUG homepage photos; every PBIMCR event has photos.
3. **Named people**: organiser bios with LinkedIn links (nor(DEV)'s Alex & Mary-Anne; Prathy Kamasani "Super Organizer" badge); speaker names and bios in full.
4. **Named sponsors with a stated reason**: London FabUG doesn't just show Experian's logo — it says *"The amazing venue at their offices in London Victoria."*
Weak groups have none of these: stock images, empty organizer sections, no numbers, logo-free.

### Pattern 3 — A first-timer promise, verbatim
PBIMCR ends every event listing with: *"**1st time attendees what to expect:** Engaging tech talks/demos/presentations and an opportunity to network in a friendly community environment with complimentary drinks and pizza served."* London FabUG: *"Please come along — you will be sure of a warm welcome"* plus exact agenda timings (18:30 welcome/pizza → 18:45 session 1 → 20:45 close) and even the practical detail that building security needs your full name. Newcomer anxiety is the #1 conversion blocker for a first visit; the strong groups answer "what will it be like?" before anyone asks.

### Pattern 4 — Every audience gets its own CTA and its own page
nor(DEV)'s nav is the cleanest statement of this: **Home · About · What to expect? · Attend · Speak · Sponsor · News.** One nav item per audience. D365PPUG puts "Are you interested in speaking at a user group?" with a Microsoft Form link on its homepage. Scottish Summit has "Download Sponsor Pack" (self-serve, no email required to start) and a Sessionize call-for-speakers with a *guidelines page for first-time submitters*. Weak groups have one CTA: "join" — which serves nobody specifically.

### Pattern 5 — The sponsor proposition is priced, tiered, and community-framed
- nor(DEV) publishes prices: Headline £6,000 (30-min speaking slot, double exhibition space, 6 tickets) / Partner £4,000 / Associate £500 / à-la-carte (refreshments £1,400, dinner £3,300, swag bag £1,000) — and justifies value with *values*: "align with a trusted, not-for-profit organisation celebrated for inclusivity & accessibility", plus published post-event financials.
- South Coast Summit runs six tiers including Lanyard and Charity sponsor slots (small-ticket entry points), and opens next year's sponsorship immediately.
- D365PPUG frames it as enablement: "Through the support of our amazing sponsors, we can host user group meetings across the UK, for free" — and asks attendees to *thank sponsors* publicly.
- DataGrillen makes tiers memorable (Grillmeister/Grillrost/Anzünder).
The weak pattern: no sponsor page at all, or logos with no path to becoming one.

### Pattern 6 — Speakers are recruited by system, not by luck
The evidence-backed speaker pipeline:
- A **permanent speaker-interest form** promoted at every event via QR code (Areti Iles: "they could be future speakers!").
- **Sessionize** for calls-for-speakers with published guidance for first-timers (Scottish Summit).
- **Mentoring for newcomers** — New Stars of Data pairs every new speaker with a veteran; DataGrillen ran a dedicated "Newcomer Track". Groups that grow their own speakers never run dry.
- **Speaker care**: promote their sessions, photograph them presenting, send them the photos, ask them to post — every strong group's event pages double as speaker marketing (PBIMCR's full bios and abstracts).

### Pattern 7 — Capture email, not just followers
LBAG (12,000 members) opens its About section with *"Please sign up for our monthly newsletter — this has advance notice and pre-registration for our events. In April 2026, we are moving off meetup."* — a 10-year-old group deliberately migrating its audience to owned email. nor(DEV) sweetens signup ("Speaker announcements & interviews, win hampers & merch"). The dead PUG-platform groups (Pattern: e-squillace list) are the cautionary tale: **audiences built on rented platforms die with the platform.**

### Pattern 8 — Operational honesty about free events
From the MVP operator playbook: free events see **40–50% no-show rates**; first events get ~20 attendees from 50 signups. Strong groups engineer around it: reminder message 1–2 weeks out, final reminder the day before ("update your RSVP if you can no longer attend"), post-event thank-you to *all* registrants (including no-shows: "join us next time!") with speaker LinkedIn links and next-event registration. The retention loop is a comms cadence, not a hope.

### Pattern 9 — Local identity is a moat
nor(DEV) is unbeatable in Norwich because it is *of* Norwich: founder story ("after experiencing London meetups, Paul saw a need in Norwich"), local charity (The Feed), local venues, walks around Eaton Park. DataGrillen made Lingen, Germany a destination with bratwurst. Generic groups ("Microsoft Fabric UK") have reach but no gravity. **NPPUG's Norfolk identity is its defensible advantage against every national/virtual alternative.**

### Pattern 10 — Content compounds; single-platform presence decays
Strong groups publish everywhere from one hub: YouTube session recordings (LBAG, D365PPUG, PBIMCR playlists), news posts (DUG publishes weekly — that's why it ranks), photo galleries, and cross-post to Meetup + LinkedIn + Microsoft Community with links back to the website. Weak groups exist on exactly one platform, and when that platform's algorithm or product changes, they vanish.

## 5. The NPPUG blueprint

### 5.1 Positioning statement (use everywhere, adapt per channel)

> **Norfolk's Microsoft community.** Free monthly meetups in Norwich for everyone working with Power Platform, Power BI, Fabric, Dynamics 365, and Copilot — from first flow to enterprise architecture. Real speakers, real demos, free pizza, no sales pitches. Organisers of the East of England Power Platform Summit.

Why: leads with local identity (Pattern 9), states cost/cadence/place (Pattern 1), names the audience spectrum (North East PBI's "who it's for" pattern), borrows EoEPPS as the credibility anchor (Pattern 2), and the "no sales pitches" promise addresses the #1 newcomer suspicion (Areti: "discourage sales-pitch sessions").

### 5.2 Homepage section order (evidence-derived)

1. **Hero = identity + next event.** Left: "Norfolk's Microsoft Power Platform community — free monthly meetups in Norwich." Right (or immediately below): the next event card — date, venue, speaker names + photos, "Register free" as the single primary button. *(Pattern 1; PBIMCR/D365PPUG)*
2. **Proof bar.** Real numbers only: "Founded 2025 · Monthly meetups · X events held · X speakers hosted · Organisers of EoEPPS · 385+ LinkedIn followers". Update quarterly. *(DUG, DataGrillen, D365PPUG counters)*
3. **Your first meetup — what to expect.** Verbatim-style promise: "Arrive 18:15, grab pizza and a drink, two 40-minute talks with a break, done by 20:45. Come alone — you won't stay alone. No experience needed, nothing to prepare, nobody will make you speak." Photo of a real meetup beside it. *(Pattern 3; PBIMCR)*
4. **Photo strip from past events.** 4–6 real photos, link to full gallery. *(nor(DEV))*
5. **What we cover → what you'll get.** Keep the product grid but reframe each tile as an outcome: "Power Apps — build your first app", "Power BI — answer questions your reports can't", "Copilot Studio — agents that actually help", "Careers — meet local employers and peers". *(Pattern: criterion 15; North East PBI copy)*
6. **Speak / Sponsor / Volunteer row.** Three cards, one CTA each: "Give your first (or fiftieth) talk — we'll help you prep" → speaker form; "Put your brand in front of Norfolk's Microsoft professionals" → sponsor page; "Help us run it" → contact. *(Pattern 4; nor(DEV) nav, D365PPUG speaker form)*
7. **Sponsors.** Logos + one line each on *why* they sponsor ("venue hosts", "pizza sponsors") + "Become a sponsor". Thank them the D365PPUG way: "Our sponsors keep every event free." *(Pattern 5; London FabUG's Experian line)*
8. **Newsletter capture.** "Get each event in your inbox first — plus speaker announcements. No spam, monthly at most." This is the page's secondary conversion everywhere the register button isn't. *(Pattern 7; LBAG, nor(DEV))*
9. **Footer:** Code of Conduct link, accessibility note, all platform links, EoEPPS link.

### 5.3 Event page template

Per event (PBIMCR/London FabUG model):
- Title with speaker names in it ("NPPUG July: Melissa Hale on Copilot Studio + Emma-Claire Shaw on Dataverse")
- Date/time, venue with parking + accessibility line, map link
- **Exact agenda with times** (18:15 doors/pizza → 18:30 welcome → 18:40 talk 1 → 19:25 break → 19:40 talk 2 → 20:30 networking → 20:45 close)
- Full session abstracts + speaker bios with LinkedIn links and photos
- "First time? What to expect" block (reuse the homepage component)
- Register CTA at top *and* bottom; dietary/access question in the registration flow (Areti)
- After the event: swap the register button for photos, slides, recording link, and "Next event →" — event pages become the SEO archive (Pattern 10)

### 5.4 Sponsor proposition (build as /sponsorship + downloadable one-pager)

Structure (nor(DEV) + SCS + Scottish Summit synthesis):
1. Who attends: roles, local employers represented, attendance numbers per meetup, LinkedIn reach, EoEPPS reach.
2. Community framing: "free events only happen because of sponsors — attendees know it and thank you for it" (D365PPUG).
3. Tiers, priced (calibrate to nor(DEV)'s local benchmarks):
   - **Annual Community Sponsor** (logo everywhere, thanked at every meetup, first refusal on EoEPPS) — the recurring backbone
   - **Venue Host** (in-kind: room + AV)
   - **Pizza Sponsor** (in-kind/cash per event, named on the night: "tonight's pizza is on X")
   - **Recruitment Partner** (the proven Meetup model: Robert Walters→PBIMCR, Nigel Frank→London PUG — recruiters *want* this audience)
4. "Download the sponsor pack" (PDF, self-serve — Scottish Summit) + contact form.
5. No sales-pitch talks except by sponsors, disclosed (Areti's rule — protects content quality while giving sponsors a real perk).

### 5.5 Speaker pipeline

- Permanent **speaker-interest form** (Microsoft Forms, like D365PPUG's), linked from homepage, every event page, and a QR slide at every meetup.
- **First-timer track**: "Never spoken before? Do 10 minutes. We'll mentor you." — the New Stars of Data model, locally. Pair each first-timer with an experienced NPPUG speaker.
- **Speaker care checklist** (from the MVP playbook): pre-event sync call, template slide with sponsor slide included, photos taken and sent after, public thanks tagging them on LinkedIn, small gift. Speakers who feel celebrated recruit the next speakers.
- List NPPUG on **Sessionize's user-group directory** to attract touring national/international speakers (how PBIMCR and London FabUG land Microsoft product-group and MVP speakers).

### 5.7 Retention loop (per event, from the MVP operator playbook)

- T-2 weeks: reminder to registrants + "update your RSVP" ask
- T-1 day: final reminder with parking/entry details
- On the night: name tags, organiser polos/tags, welcome slide reel (agenda, sponsor, next-event QR, speaker-form QR), icebreaker for early arrivals, closing slide promoting next event *and other local groups* (nor(DEV), EoEPPS — generosity is a growth strategy)
- T+1 day: thank-you to **all** registrants including no-shows ("sorry we missed you — here's what's next"), speaker LinkedIn links, photos, next-event registration link
- T+3 days: LinkedIn recap post tagging speakers, venue, sponsors
- Plan for 40–50% no-show on free tickets; overbook the room accordingly

### 5.8 Norfolk-specific moves

1. **Tell the founder story** on /about the way nor(DEV) does: why Norfolk needed this, who you are, LinkedIn links, faces. (Duncan's bio already does this well on the nor(DEV) event listing — put it on NPPUG's own site.)
2. **Ally with nor(DEV)**, don't compete: cross-promote, guest-speak (already happening — the "AI for Power BI Professionals" talk), share their closing-slide generosity.
3. **Own "Microsoft community East of England"** in search: NPPUG (monthly) + EoEPPS (annual) is the same funnel — put each at the top of the other's site. EoEPPS's "Returning in 2027" page should point at NPPUG as "the community between summits" so the summit audience feeds the meetup year-round (fixes the South Coast Summit off-season problem).
4. **A local charity partnership** (nor(DEV) × The Feed model) — one collection per year converts goodwill into local-press credibility.
5. **Adopt a Code of Conduct** (copy the PBIMCR or nor(DEV) pattern) and a one-line accessibility statement per venue.
